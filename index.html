Timeline · Tongaat Hulett

cane titan
to collapse?

With roots dating back to the 19th century and a listing in 1952, Tongaat Hulett currently has three sugar mills operating on the north coast of KwaZulu-Natal. It employs some 2 500 people directly, with some 3 500 growers, about 500 which are commercial scale, supplying the mills. Tongaat’s name itself is derived from a river, the 'uThongathi' River. The town of Tongaat name was officially changed from the anglicised version to, oThongathi, in 2010, and a liquidation of SA’s storied sugar giant is expected to economically and socially devastate the area.

1850s
The Huletts Corporation takes root
The origins of one of SA's most storied sugar dynasties begin in the 1850s.

The Huletts Corporation had its beginnings in the 1850s, with the original incorporation in 1892 as Sir J L Hulett and Sons — laying the foundation for what would become one of South Africa's most significant agri-processing businesses.

1875
Founding
1875
The Tongaat Group is born
A partnership between Edward Saunders and W.J. Mirrlees gives rise to the Tongaat Group.

The Tongaat Group began in 1875 as a partnership between Edward Saunders and W.J. Mirrlees, and was incorporated in 1892 as Tongaat Sugar in Pretoria. The name itself derives from the uThongathi River — the town of Tongaat was officially renamed oThongathi in 2010.

1962
Milestone
1962
Anglo American enters the picture
The mining giant buys its first shares in Tongaat — a relationship that would last nearly five decades.

Anglo American buys its first shares in Tongaat in 1962 and held an interest of more than 50% between 1998 and 2009, when it sold its shareholding in the company — ending nearly five decades of involvement in the sugar group.

1969–70
Milestone
1969–1970
A diversified industrial empire
At its peak diversification, the Tongaat Group spans aluminium, textiles, sugar, cotton, edible oils and more.

The Tongaat Group operated a diversified industrial business with interests spanning aluminium, building materials, consumer foods, cotton, edible oils, industrial and commercial catering, mushrooms, sugar and agricultural land development, starch and glucose, textiles and transport.

1982
Milestone
1982
Tongaat and Hulett merge
The Tongaat-Hulett Group is formed, creating one of SA's most powerful agri-processing conglomerates.

The Tongaat-Hulett Group was formed in 1982 when the Tongaat Group merged with Hulett's — uniting two of KwaZulu-Natal's most significant sugar and industrial businesses under one banner.

2007
Milestone
2007
Hulamin unbundled
The group sheds its aluminium arm, listing Hulamin as an independent producer.

The Tongaat-Hulett Group unbundles Hulamin — an independent niche producer of aluminium rolled, extruded and other semi-fabricated and finished products — as it begins refocusing its business around its core sugar and agricultural land interests.

2008
Milestone
2008
Tongaat Hulett Limited — a focused agri-processor
A new name and a sharpened focus on sugar, land management and property development.

Tongaat Hulett becomes a focused agri-processing business with integrated components of land management, property development and agriculture. The group's name formally changes to Tongaat Hulett Limited to reflect its renewed strategic direction.

2019
Crisis
2019
R3.5bn accounting fraud — SA's second biggest after Steinhoff
Tongaat requests a JSE suspension after discovering a massive accounting scandal. A PwC probe fingers 10 executives.

Tongaat requests a suspension from the JSE after discovering an alleged R3.5 billion accounting fraud — South Africa's second biggest corporate scandal after Steinhoff. The company embarks on a turnaround process but struggles with a crippling local debt pile.

As part of the turnaround, Tongaat Hulett disposes of its sugar operations in Namibia and Eswatini, and its starch business in SA (now Barloworld's Ingrain). A PwC investigation points the finger at 10 executives for, among other allegations, profit inflation.

"SA's second biggest accounting scandal after Steinhoff — an alleged R3.5 billion in fraudulent profit inflation."

— PwC investigation findings, 2019

Crisis
2022
Business rescue — lenders lose patience
After years of turnaround attempts disrupted in part by the KZN riots, Tongaat enters business rescue.

Tongaat enters business rescue after lenders lose patience with the pace of the turnaround. The company's recovery efforts — which had included ambitious land-sale plans — were in part disrupted by the devastating KwaZulu-Natal riots of 2021.

Jan 2024
Legal
January 2024
Creditors approve Vision's rescue plan — but RGS cries foul
A rival Mozambican consortium pulls its plan at the last minute, then alleges bias — and a R2bn bank letter turns out to be fake.

Tongaat creditors approve a plan put forward by a consortium called Vision. A rival plan from Mozambican consortium RGS is pulled at the last minute. RGS alleges bias in the process — denied by Tongaat's rescue practitioners.

It also emerges that a R2 billion bank letter submitted to show proof of funding was faked. RGS denies wrongdoing, saying it was an unfortunate mishap that followed a junior employee moving the funds to another account.

2024–2026
RGS launches a wave of court challenges
The rival consortium questions whether Vision has the funding to implement its own plan — and challenges its legality in court.

RGS launches a series of court challenges, alleging the Vision plan is unlawful and questioning whether it had the funding to implement its own plan. The legal wrangling significantly complicates and delays the business rescue process.

Aug 2024
Crisis
August 2024
Shareholders shoot down debt-for-equity swap
The rejection kicks off an alternative sales process — and likely seals the company's JSE delisting.

Tongaat shareholders shoot down a proposed debt-for-equity swap, kicking off an alternative sales process by which operations in SA, Mozambique, Botswana and Zimbabwe are to be sold separately. The move likely seals the company's JSE delisting.

Jun 2025
Crisis
June 2025
SA sale deadline passes — talks drag on
The intended deadline for the sale of the SA business, where R12bn in debt sits, passes without resolution.

The intended deadline for the sale of the SA business — where the group's approximately R12 billion debt pile sits — passes without a deal being concluded.

Discussions over increased post-commencement funding between Vision, the lender group, and the SA government continue. Low sugar prices and increasing imports further complicate the situation.

Dec 2025
Legal
December 2025
Tongaat loses Supreme Court of Appeal sugar levy battle
The courts rule that sugar levy obligations are statutory — and business rescue cannot shield Tongaat from them.

Tongaat loses its Supreme Court of Appeal battle over the payment of its sugar levy obligations — another major blow. The company had argued that business rescue proceedings should shield it from these obligations. The courts have concluded the levies are statutory in nature and therefore obligatory.

Tongaat's business rescue practitioners file for liquidation, saying that Vision's demands for sugar industry reforms were not part of the approved rescue plan, and that the plan is no longer implementable.
